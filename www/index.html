Agreement is not the same as knowledge.

A side-by-side map of classical Byzantine Fault Tolerance and a type-augmented design. Toggle stages, failure overlays, and claims. This is a teaching comparator—not a claim that dependent types replace a network protocol.

1 · Propose

A value is broadcast. X may equivocate, omit, or lie.

2 · Vote / prepare

Replicas exchange protocol messages and collect votes. Safety depends on quorum intersection and the fault model.

3 · Commit

With a valid quorum, replicas decide. Liveness may wait for synchrony; BFT does not prove the value is true.

Guarantee: agreement (under stated assumptions). Not guaranteed: factual correctness of an authenticated sender’s claim.

1 · Construct

A value travels with an evidence object, e.g. Verified origin timestamp; a type can require the witness.

2 · Check

The boundary validates the evidence and its scope. An attacker can still send a signed lie unless the predicate captures the relevant truth.

3 · Admit or reject

Invalid evidence is rejected locally—or retained as an explicit Unverified / Echo value. This complements, rather than magically replaces, consensus.

Guarantee: no unchecked claim crosses a typed boundary (if the implementation and specification are sound). Not guaranteed: that the sensor or predicate is truthful.

What the red overlay means: A faulty sender can equivocate. BFT contains this through quorum rules; types reject only what their predicates can detect. Authentication proves who spoke, not that they spoke truth.

Where this repo fits

This checkout is a Robodog ECM/safety lab with Idris ABI declarations, Rust implementation/tests, and SPARK safety modules. The strongest immediate connection is evidence at safety boundaries: make detection, formation, and autonomy outputs distinguish measured, inferred, stale, degraded, and rejected.

Idris2 model evidence/indexed states and protocol shape. Rust enforce explicit enums and rejection paths. SPARK prove safety properties of the boundary—not physical truth.

  3. Model a 3f+1 quorum trace beside the typed trace; inject counterexamples.
  4. Measure cost: latency, memory, proof/check overhead, false rejection.
  5. Only then connect Echo, epistemic, tropical, choreography, residual evidence, Januskey, or Absolute Zero as named modules with operational semantics and tests.

Reading guide: BFT answers “can non-faulty replicas agree despite faults?” Typed evidence answers “what states may this component accept, and why?” Those are orthogonal questions. Keep the distinction visible.
